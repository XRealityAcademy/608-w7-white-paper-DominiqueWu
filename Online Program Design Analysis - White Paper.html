Trends analyzed

Online learning programs in U.S. higher education are at a turning point. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated digital adoption, and sustained pressures around equity, completion, and workforce alignment continue to reshape how institutions design, deliver, and evaluate online programs.

This white paper analyzes how nine contemporary trends and issues are positively shaping the design of the California Virtual Campus (CVC), the system-wide online education program of the California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office (CCCCO).

Drawing on the Changing Landscape of Online Education (CHLOE) report (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024), the National Standards for Quality Online Programs (National Standards for Quality Online Learning, n.d.), and the Online Learning Consortium Quality Scorecard (Online Learning Consortium, n.d.), the analysis covers the six required areas of learner support, curriculum and course design, assessment and learner performance, equity and access, faculty and staff support, and technology support.

It also covers three additional areas of program evaluation, leadership, and financial and material resources. For each area, the paper identifies a current trend, explains how it contributes to program success, and recommends a strategy for further capitalizing on it.

The findings indicate that the CVC's investments in standardized course design, Zero Textbook Cost pathways, and inter-segmental partnerships are paying off, and that the system can extend its leadership by deepening analytics, faculty development, and immersive technology readiness.

Framework · The intentional design that holds the paper together

Three pillars of intentional online program design.

Cavanagh (2020) argues that high-performing online programs are not the sum of well-designed courses; they are coherent systems built on three interlocking pillars. The CVC was analyzed against this anchor, with each of the nine areas mapping to one or more pillars.

PILLAR · 01

Course

Standardized, rubric-aligned design at the unit level. Every learner meets a consistent baseline of clarity, alignment, and accessibility.

  • POCR-reviewed exchange courses
  • OEI Course Design Rubric (QM-aligned)
  • Read · Watch · Apply · Reflect template
  • Authentic, formative assessment mix
PILLAR · 02

Support

Wraparound services that meet learners where they are, embedded in the same platform where they take their classes, around the clock.

  • NetTutor & embedded library widgets
  • Online counseling & mental-health partners
  • Predictive analytics + success coaches
  • 24/7 tech support & accessibility tools
PILLAR · 03

Program

Coherence above the course, pathways, governance, evaluation, and resources that hold the system together as a single offering.

  • NSQ & OLC Scorecard program review
  • Vision 2030 inter-segmental governance
  • JCSEE-aligned evaluation framework
  • State funding & CVC innovation fund
Throughline "Programs that are designed, supported, and evaluated as coherent systems consistently outperform those that emerge as the sum of their courses." Cavanagh, 2020

Section 01 · Setting the table

Introduction.

The California Community Colleges (CCC) system serves more than 1.8 million students across 116 colleges, making it the largest system of higher education in the United States. Within the system, the California Virtual Campus (CVC), administered by the CCCCO in partnership with Foothill De Anza, has become the central online education infrastructure connecting students to fully online courses and degrees across colleges.

The CVC offers a shared course exchange, a common Canvas learning management system instance, and quality processes such as Peer Online Course Review (POCR) (California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, 2024).

This white paper analyzes how trends and issues identified through national and state-level research contribute positively to the design of the CVC. It uses the National Standards for Quality (NSQ) Online Programs (National Standards for Quality Online Learning, n.d.) and the Online Learning Consortium Quality Scorecard (Online Learning Consortium, n.d.) as scaffolding, with supplemental data from the CHLOE report (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024) and Modernizing Learning (Walcutt & Schatz, 2019).

The analysis covers nine areas, six of which are required by the assignment and three of which were selected because they have the strongest current impact on the CVC. Each section identifies a trend or issue, explains its contribution to program success, and offers a recommendation for taking further advantage of it.

02
Section 02 · How we got here

Methodology.

Trends and issues were identified by triangulating three sources. First, the CHLOE report, the most comprehensive annual survey of online learning leaders in the United States, supplied current data on enrollment patterns, faculty support, and course design practices (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024).

Second, the NSQ Online Programs rubric provided the program areas used to organize the analysis (National Standards for Quality Online Learning, n.d.).

Third, the OLC Quality Scorecard for the Administration of Online Programs supplied additional benchmarking criteria (Online Learning Consortium, n.d.). The CVC was selected as the program of analysis because it represents a state-level online program with a substantial public footprint and an active commitment to evidence-based design.

Part I · Six required NSQ program areas

The six trends California is already capitalizing on.

01Required

Learner Support

Advising, counseling, and wraparound services

Trend

A persistent CHLOE finding is that online learners require, and increasingly receive, comprehensive support services that mirror or exceed those of on-campus students, including online tutoring, counseling, library services, and career advising (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024).

Contribution

The CVC's integration of services such as NetTutor, online counseling appointments, and Canvas-embedded library widgets has measurably increased course completion and reduced equity gaps. Wraparound support is correlated with retention because online learners often face logistical barriers, including work, caregiving, and transportation, that traditional support hours cannot accommodate (Walcutt & Schatz, 2019). When learners can access advising, tutoring, and mental health services from within the same platform where they take their classes, friction is reduced and persistence improves.

Recommend

Expand proactive outreach by deploying predictive learning analytics that flag at-risk students within the first three weeks of a course and trigger personalized outreach from an online success coach. Pair this with extended mental health partnerships so that psychosocial support keeps pace with academic intervention. Adopting a single sign-on across tutoring, counseling, and library platforms would further reduce friction for learners.

02Required

Curriculum and Course Design

Standardized, rubric-aligned design

Trend

Standardized, rubric-aligned course design has emerged as a defining feature of high-quality online programs (Cavanagh, 2020; Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024).

Contribution

The CVC's @ONE-administered Peer Online Course Review (POCR) process, aligned with the Online Education Initiative Course Design Rubric (a state-customized adaptation of Quality Matters standards), has produced more than 1,500 quality-reviewed courses available through the course exchange. Faculty who complete POCR report stronger alignment among learning objectives, content, activities, and assessments, which is the heart of Cavanagh's (2020) intentional design framework. This consistency benefits students who take courses across multiple colleges through the exchange, because they encounter a similar quality baseline regardless of their home college.

Recommend

Move from course-level review to program-level review by piloting a CVC Program Quality Scorecard aligned with NSQ Online Programs (National Standards for Quality Online Learning, n.d.), so that pathways and degree maps are evaluated for coherence, not just individual courses. Embedding a common module template, with read, watch, apply, and reflect components, across exchange courses would also strengthen the learner experience.

Assessment and Learner Performance

Authentic, formative, and competency-based

Trend

A shift away from high-stakes summative testing toward authentic, formative, and competency-based assessment is a defining feature of future-focused learning ecosystems (Walcutt & Schatz, 2019).

Contribution

Across the CVC, faculty are integrating low-stakes weekly quizzes, performance tasks, and ePortfolio-based assessments to capture deeper learning. This aligns with the recommendation in Modernizing Learning to decrease reliance on high-stakes assessments while increasing formative and experiential measures (Walcutt & Schatz, 2019). Authentic assessment also reduces the integrity risks associated with online testing, because students are evaluated through artifacts that demonstrate their learning rather than through one-time, easily compromised exams.

Recommend

Develop a system-wide ePortfolio infrastructure that travels with students across colleges, supporting credit for prior learning, micro-credential validation, and competency mapping for transfer to California State University (CSU) and the University of California (UC) systems. Pair this with a CVC assessment framework that requires every online program to publish its mix of formative, authentic, and summative measures.

04Required

Equity and Access

OER & Zero Textbook Cost pathways

Trend

Open Educational Resources (OER) and Zero Textbook Cost (ZTC) degree pathways are among the most widely adopted equity strategies in U.S. higher education, with California leading through Assembly Bill 798 and Assembly Bill 132 funding (California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, 2024).

Contribution

ZTC pathways remove a significant financial barrier for low-income students and have been linked to higher enrollment, persistence, and completion. Stories from open advocates illustrate why equity-focused practices have spread so quickly, financial barriers are learning barriers, and OER offers a sustainable, scalable response (see callout, right).

Recommend

Expand ZTC beyond two-year degrees by partnering with CSU and UC to align ZTC transfer pathways, and incentivize faculty OER authoring through dedicated time, stipends, and tenure credit recognition. Extending the open ethos into emerging immersive technologies (AR and VR) would also help ensure that the next wave of educational innovation remains accessible to every learner, not just those at well-resourced institutions.

05Required

Faculty and Staff Support

Online teaching PD & instructional designer partnerships

Trend

Investment in online teaching professional development and instructional designer partnerships is a hallmark of mature online programs (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024).

Contribution

The CCCCO's @ONE professional development network offers free certification pathways, courses, and webinars in online teaching, accessibility, and equity-minded design, reaching thousands of faculty annually. This built capacity allowed the system to pivot quickly during the pandemic and to sustain quality afterward. Faculty who participate in @ONE programs are also more likely to engage in POCR, creating a virtuous cycle between professional development and course quality.

Recommend

Establish a system-wide instructional designer cohort embedded in each district to provide ongoing course-design partnership and to scale POCR participation. Tie advancement to documented online teaching competency, and create a CVC Faculty Fellows program that recognizes excellence in online pedagogy and provides release time for mentorship and curriculum development.

06Required

Technology Support

24/7 support & accessibility tools as baseline

Trend

Integrated 24/7 technical support combined with accessibility tools (such as Ally and ReadSpeaker) has become a baseline expectation for online programs (Online Learning Consortium, n.d.).

Contribution

The CVC's common Canvas instance simplifies tech support across the system and enables shared accessibility tools. Centralized technology removes friction, particularly for students enrolled at multiple colleges through the course exchange, and it ensures that accessibility is treated as a system feature rather than as an individual faculty responsibility.

Recommend

Pilot AI-assisted tech support that offers real-time troubleshooting and accessibility recommendations within Canvas, and build immersive technology readiness (AR and VR) into the technology roadmap to align with CCCCO Vision 2030 spatial computing initiatives. A shared AR and VR content repository would also lower the barrier for faculty experimentation.

07Selected

Program Evaluation

Continuous improvement powered by analytics & peer rubrics

Trend

Continuous program improvement powered by learning analytics and peer-validated rubrics is an emerging strength in mature online systems (Quality Matters & Eduventures, 2024).

Contribution

The CVC uses NSQ-aligned indicators and OLC Scorecard reviews to benchmark progress. Annual reports inform funding decisions and expansion priorities, and the Vision 2030 framework establishes shared metrics across colleges (California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, 2024). This evidence-based approach gives the CVC credibility with state legislators, regional accreditors, and partner segments.

Recommend

Adopt a Joint Committee on Standards for Educational Evaluation (JCSEE) aligned program evaluation framework to ensure that evaluations meet the utility, feasibility, propriety, accuracy, and accountability standards expected of professional evaluation. Layer this framework on top of existing NSQ and OLC reviews to strengthen the credibility of CVC reporting with policymakers and accreditors.

08Selected

Leadership

Distributed, inter-segmental governance

Trend

Distributed and inter-segmental leadership models are shaping large online systems (Walcutt & Schatz, 2019).

Contribution

Through Vision 2030, the CCCCO has partnered with CSU and UC on shared transfer pathways, OER investments, and learning analytics. This distributed leadership reduces silos and aligns goals across the three public segments of California higher education. It also creates a stable governance structure that survives changes in individual leadership, which is critical for a multi-year initiative.

Recommend

Formalize a Vision 2030 Online Learning Leadership Council with rotating chair seats from each college and inter-segmental partner, ensuring sustained momentum. Publish an annual State of Online Learning report co-authored across CCC, CSU, and UC to maintain shared visibility into outcomes, gaps, and priorities.

09Selected

Financial and Material Resources

State legislation, grants, and a system-level funding model

Trend

Dedicated state legislation and grant programs are funding online learning infrastructure at unprecedented levels (California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, 2024).

Contribution

California's Equitable Placement, ZTC, and Strong Workforce funds have allowed the CVC to invest in shared technology, professional development, and equity initiatives without burdening individual colleges. This system-level funding model spreads cost and risk, making it possible for smaller colleges to access enterprise-grade resources they could not afford individually.

Recommend

Diversify funding by pursuing federal grants and philanthropic partnerships that target immersive technology, AI literacy, and workforce alignment, ensuring sustainability beyond state appropriations. Establish a CVC innovation fund that allows colleges to pilot emerging practices, with a clear path to scaling successful pilots system-wide.

Conclusion · The throughline

Intentionality, at scale, is the defining challenge of the next decade.

Conclusion.

The California Virtual Campus exemplifies how a state-level online program can leverage contemporary trends, such as standardized course design, OER, integrated learner support, distributed leadership, and analytics-driven evaluation, to advance equitable and scalable online education. Each of the nine areas analyzed in this paper shows clear evidence of trend-driven success, but each also points to opportunities for further growth.

By extending these strengths into program-level review, immersive technology, and JCSEE-aligned evaluation, the CCCCO can position the CVC as a national model for the next decade of online learning.

"The throughline across all nine areas is intentionality: programs that are designed, supported, and evaluated as coherent systems consistently outperform those that emerge as the sum of their courses." CAVANAGH, 2020

Sustaining that intentionality at scale will be the defining challenge, and opportunity, of the next era of online learning in California.
